High Country Couture

Eden Hore's story, told in fabric. House of Eden is a creative play on the textiles, colour and pattern of the Eden Hore collection: the couture a Central Otago sheep farmer gathered from New Zealand's leading designers.

Inside the house

Three spaces, one story.

Space one · The centrepiece

Colour Field

A loose framework shaped like the swish of a dress. Projectors play the collection's fabrics across silhouettes, mirrors and visitors themselves, so everyone becomes part of the collection.

Projection · silhouettes · mirrors · landscape backdrop

Space two · Hands on

Design your own dress

A patchwork wall of prints and textures, and a table where visitors pick a silhouette and fabric to design a dress of their own.

Florals · animal print · geometrics · leather · lamé

Space three · Where the collection was kept

The Tractor Shed

On Glenshee Station, Eden's couture was stored in the tractor shed. This space recreates it with reclaimed timber, sliding doors and era furniture. Visitors sit and watch the Eden Hore documentary in the place where the story was kept.

Why us

The shed that started everything.

I was eight when I first saw Eden's collection, kept in a tractor shed on a sheep farm near Naseby.

I didn't have the words for it then: the colour, the drama, the care he'd taken with every piece. It stayed with me through years of sewing, alterations and dressmaking, and forty years later it became Lohi.

Russ and I came to Ranfurly because we loved the place. We restored the old drapery on Charlemont Street, and two garments from Eden's collection are on display there today: one by Vinka Lucas, and one by Pat Hewitt of Alexandra, whose One Only label made one-off pieces. House of Eden is how we give other people the moment I had in that shed.

Eden's story

Ball gowns from a sheep station.

Eden Hore (1919–1997) farmed Glenshee Station near Naseby. Through the 1970s and 1980s he built one of New Zealand's most unexpected fashion collections: more than 270 garments and accessories.

It began with wool and leather and grew into evening wear, bridal and couture. After his death the family kept the collection together. It is now held by the Central Otago District Council.

House of Eden is one stop on a wider Eden Hore story being built across Otago, from Eden in Dunedin at Toitū Otago Settlers Museum through Central Otago to Queenstown. We work with the Council under a memorandum of understanding and run House of Eden independently, under their guidance on the care of the garments.

Our story

I was eight when I first walked into Eden Hore's tractor shed.

He was a sheep farmer near Naseby with an eye for beauty, and the shed was full of couture. I didn't have the words for it then, but I never forgot it.

I grew up around sewing machines and ran an alteration and dressmaking business for years. It took forty years for what that shed planted to become Lohi.

Russ and I moved to Ranfurly because we loved it. We restored the old drapery on Charlemont Street and opened Lohi Linen and The Thirsty Moa. Two garments from Eden's collection are on display at Lohi today, one by Vinka Lucas and one by Pat Hewitt of Alexandra under her One Only label, and we hold a memorandum of understanding with the Council to tell his story.

House of Eden is the next step, and its third space is the tractor shed. We want every visitor to have the moment I had at eight.

Tania and Russ

The invitation

We're looking for a few people to build this with us: a small group of investors, ideally tourism operators who can bring visitors as well as capital, to take a 49% stake in House of Eden Ltd. Lemon Fix Ltd, which runs Lohi Linen, The Thirsty Moa and rail trail accommodation on Charlemont Street, keeps 51% and runs the business.

The collection is held by the Central Otago District Council. House of Eden operates independently as a commercial business, under the Council's guidance, and sits within the Council's wider Eden Hore trail from Dunedin to Queenstown.

The experience is designed by Art of Fact as a creative play on fabric and storytelling. Watch the concept film above for the walk-through.
